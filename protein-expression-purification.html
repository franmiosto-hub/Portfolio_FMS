Protein expression, purification and characterization

Every computational result upstream of this page depends on one thing: a sample that is pure, folded and correctly quantified. Around five years of protein production across several systems and institutions, roughly ten constructs across four to five protein systems, and — on the PhD work — the RNA side of the same problem, where the bottleneck turned out to be.

Workflow

Construct design as the main lever

RNA-binding proteins are awkward expression targets: frequently disordered in part, highly basic, prone to co-purifying with host nucleic acid, and liable to aggregate at the concentrations biophysics demands. Most of the gain comes from design decisions taken before any culture is grown.

Across the FUS and FMRP work this meant codon optimisation, solubility-tag choice and tag placement, changed expression conditions, and truncation and purity control — iterated against what the downstream assay actually required. On one construct this design and workflow optimisation produced an approximately tenfold yield improvement. Full-length FMRP expression and purification was established after redesign; the subsequent decision was deliberately not to overextend it as the primary assay system, because a protein you can make is not automatically the protein your experiment should be built on.

  1. Construct and boundary designDomain boundaries informed by disorder prediction and sequence analysis; codon optimisation, tag choice and placement, truncation strategy — selected against the downstream measurement, not against ease of purification.
  2. Expression and condition optimisationStrain, temperature and induction conditions scored on soluble yield. Bacterial systems throughout; mammalian expression was attempted during the Graz placement and did not succeed.
  3. Capture and purificationAffinity capture through to polishing — Ni-NTA (including peristaltic-pump setups) and ÄKTA/FPLC, size exclusion, SDS-PAGE at every stage. A260/A280 tracked throughout, since co-purified nucleic acid silently inflates apparent concentration and destroys binding stoichiometry.
  4. RNA productionThe other half of the problem: in vitro transcription workflows, plasmid linearisation, and domain-specific constructs including long and GC-rich sequences.
  5. VerificationIdentity by intact-protein TOF-MS and peptide fingerprinting; purity by gel; fold and stability by circular dichroism and thermal-stability measurement; size and homogeneity by SEC and SEC-MALS.
  6. Handover to assayConcentration and method, A260/A280, SEC profile, gel, and buffer composition recorded — buffer mismatch shows up later as an ITC baseline artefact or a phantom NMR shift.

The bottleneck, named

The RNA side, not the protein side, is what limited the project. RNA yield and purity — particularly for long and GC-rich sequences — proved insufficient to support the quantitative combinatorial binding assays originally planned. Identifying that bottleneck explicitly, rather than continuing to generate underpowered binding data, is what redirected the work toward the array and simulation routes.

Structural biophysics alongside

Earlier placements in Marseille and Graz covered the wider characterisation toolkit hands-on: ITC with thermal-stability-guided condition optimisation, SEC-MALS, crystallisation and diffraction testing, SAXS measurement, processing and structural docking, CD and DLS, thermal-shift and activity assays, and EM sample preparation. Several of these produced negative or uninterpretable results — salt crystals, protein precipitation, unsuccessful mammalian expression — which is where the operational knowledge came from.

Analysis that runs alongside the bench work

The quantitative parts are scripted rather than done by eye: extinction coefficients and molecular weights computed from sequence with Biopython, SEC calibration curves fitted and inverted for apparent molecular weight, gel lanes integrated for purity, thermal-melt traces fitted for a Tm, and residue-level properties — disorder, charge, van der Waals volume — computed to explain why a given construct behaves as it does.

from Bio.SeqUtils.ProtParam import ProteinAnalysis

pa  = ProteinAnalysis(sequence)
eps = pa.molar_extinction_coefficient()[1]   # reduced / cystine forms
mw  = pa.molecular_weight()
pI  = pa.isoelectric_point()

conc_M   = a280 / (eps * pathlength)
conc_mgml = conc_M * mw / 1000
purity_A = a260 / a280            # > 0.8 → nucleic-acid contamination

Scope note

This is protein-science and developability-adjacent work — aggregation and solubility, thermal and conformational stability, sequence liabilities, expression and manufacturability optimisation — carried out in a research setting. It is not industrial antibody-platform or GMP process ownership, and the depth varies by method: several of the biophysical techniques above reflect hands-on early-career execution rather than long-term platform ownership.
