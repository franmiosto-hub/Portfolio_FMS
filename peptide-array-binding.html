Peptide array binding and analysis

A custom 1,445-sequence peptide array, designed from additive modelling of published data and measured across multiple RNA conditions. The analytical problem is not detection but deconvolution under uncertainty: turning a field of fluorescent spots into residue-level contributions, while establishing how far those contributions can honestly be pushed.

Design

The array was modelled before it was printed

The library design was itself an analysis. Additive modelling of published binding data was used to choose which 1,445 sequences would be informative — spanning composition space widely enough to constrain a residue-level model, rather than sampling sequences of independent interest. Design and analysis share the same model, which is what makes the resulting fit interpretable.

Principle

An additive residue-level model

If residue contributions are approximately independent and additive, the signal for a peptide is the sum of its residue terms. Across the library this becomes an overdetermined linear system — a composition matrix times an unknown contribution vector against the observed intensities — solved as bounded-variable least squares, so that physically meaningful bounds on the coefficients are imposed as part of the fit rather than checked afterwards.

A · w ≈ y

A : n_peptides × 20   composition (or positional) matrix
y : n_peptides × 1    normalised, QC-filtered fluorescence
w : 20 × 1            per-residue contribution

w* = argmin ||A·w - y||²   subject to   lb ≤ w ≤ ub     (BVLS)

Bounds matter more than they look. An unconstrained fit will happily assign large compensating positive and negative coefficients to residues that co-occur in the library, producing a well-fitting model made of physically meaningless terms. Constraining the admissible range keeps the solution in the region where the coefficients can be read as contributions at all.

Why the pre-processing decides the result

The solver step is the easy part; everything upstream of it determines whether the answer means anything. Saturation at the top of the dynamic range, spatial gradients across the slide, empty-spot effects, spot morphology, adjacency effects between neighbouring features, and channel bleed-through all masquerade as sequence preference. Each was assessed explicitly before any model was fitted.

Pipeline

  1. Library designAdditive modelling of published data to select 1,445 sequences that span composition space informatively.
  2. Parse the .gal layoutRecover which peptide sits at which block/row/column, joining scanner output to sequence identity rather than coordinates.
  3. High-throughput quality controlSignal robustness, concentration consistency, spatial bias, empty-spot effects, spot morphology, adjacency, and composition-correlated artefacts — assessed systematically across the whole array, not spot-checked.
  4. Dual-channel normalisationF635 (Cy5, target) against F532 (Cy3, peptide-density reference), so signal reflects binding rather than how much peptide was successfully printed.
  5. BVLS deconvolutionBuild the composition matrix and solve for bounded residue contributions, with cross-validation across held-out peptide subsets.
  6. Interpretation and visualisationContribution tables, grouped plots by residue chemistry, cross-condition heatmaps, per-target regressions, and residual analysis.

Where the data stopped supporting the model

Later array runs showed a deterioration in quality that the QC surfaced clearly. The decision taken was to restrict the interpretation to what the good runs supported rather than force quantitative conclusions out of compromised arrays — the initial measurements did contain reproducible, sequence-dependent relative preferences, and that is what the analysis reports.

Two further limits are stated rather than smoothed over. Additivity cannot capture cooperativity between adjacent residues, peptide secondary structure, or avidity from multivalent presentation — so residuals are reported alongside the coefficients, and badly predicted peptides are surfaced as informative rather than discarded. And the result is explicitly not a universal affinity scale: it is a set of relative, condition-dependent preferences with a stated domain of validity.
