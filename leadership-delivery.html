Programme delivery

The doctoral programme, as a delivery problem

A PhD is usually read as a credential. This one is better read as four years of running a multi-part technical programme with real dependencies, external partners, and branches that had to be cut.

Multiscale protein–RNA programme

University of Vienna / Max Perutz Labs, Vienna BioCenter

2022 – 2026

Four connected workstreams — DOSY NMR, a custom peptide-array platform, protein and RNA production workflows, and molecular dynamics with a custom free-energy framework — each depending on different expertise, instruments and institutions, and each feeding a single scientific question.

Scope

Four technical workstreams spanning wet lab, high-throughput measurement, and HPC simulation, from experimental design through to interpretation.

Coordination

Approximately five specialist groups across Max Perutz Labs, BOKU, KIT, the University of Arizona and Vienna BioCenter Core Facilities — NMR, peptide arrays, MD/FEP, protein technologies and mass spectrometry.

Outcome

A completed monographic thesis that holds the four scales together and states the boundary conditions of each, rather than forcing a single universal claim.

4 workstreams ~5 collaborating groups ~35,000 words, monographic ~1 µs MD per complex 1,445 array sequences

Changing route without losing the objective

2023 development plan → 2026 thesis

the part that matters

The programme as proposed in 2023 is not the programme that was delivered. Several branches proved technically constrained — RNA yield and purity could not support the quantitative combinatorial binding assays; the more dynamic protein–RNA complexes turned out to be unsuitable for straightforward free-energy perturbation; later peptide-array runs deteriorated in quality.

In each case the decision was to redirect the method and preserve the question, and to report the constraint as a finding rather than absorb it silently. Knowing where a workflow stops being valid — and saying so before someone builds on it — is the transferable skill here.

Decisions taken

Restricted array interpretation to the runs that supported it. Declined to overextend full-length FMRP as the primary assay system after establishing it. Named the RNA bottleneck rather than generating underpowered data around it.

What was preserved

The core scientific question, the collaboration architecture, and the multiscale structure of the programme.

What it evidences

Scope management under technical constraint, and a willingness to report a negative result upward rather than defend a plan past its evidence.

Summary

What this adds up to

Coordination across boundaries

Institutions, disciplines, core facilities, volunteers and external stakeholders — repeatedly, in five countries, without formal authority over most of the people involved.

Delivery under constraint

Objective-preserving adaptation: changing method, scope or collaborator when the evidence demands it, while keeping the underlying goal fixed.

Honest escalation

Reporting limits, bottlenecks and negative results early, in a form the next person can act on — rather than defending a plan past the point the data supports it.

Capability left behind

New workflows, analysis pipelines, documented failure modes, collaboration interfaces and reproducible handovers — the team can do more afterwards, not just know more.
